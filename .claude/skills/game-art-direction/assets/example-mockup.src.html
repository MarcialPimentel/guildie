The short version

Five changes, one direction

1. A new setting

Portovela, a merchant republic on a cliff coast. Guilds, workshops and a busy harbor market, around 1500, in the age of invention.

2. A new palette

Sea teal, whitewash, lemon and roof-tile terracotta. It is light, sunny and nothing like Hormiga's brass and soot.

3. A world, not a spreadsheet

Every system becomes a place with a verb: you pump the forge, empty the cart, set out your stall and meet the ships.

4. 3D in the browser

Keep TypeScript and the server. Add Three.js for the world and keep HTML for menus. No Unity, no rewrite of the game logic.

5. Art we can actually make

Chunky low-poly models sharing one palette texture, from free kits and AI 3D tools, cleaned up and wired in by me.

Setting

Two ways to go Italian

Today the game's story is "medieval apprentice rises into the steam age". Steam is built into the rank names, the Steam Engine building, coal as a cost, and item names like Steam Tonic. An Italian merchant theme can either replace steam or keep it.

Recommended

A. Renaissance merchant republic

Around 1500, a city like Amalfi, Venice or Genoa. Steam is replaced by the age of invention: water wheels, gear trains and Leonardo-style machines that keep working while you're away.

  • Guilds, workshops and the apprentice-to-master path are Italian history. Florence's guilds were literally called Arti, and the bottega was the workshop where apprentices trained.
  • Merchant republics invented banking, bookkeeping and the trading exchange. Venice's Rialto is the auction-house fantasy.
  • Costs a flavor pass over names and the rank story. Game rules stay the same.
Low-change option

B. Riviera in the age of steam

Around 1890, a pastel coastal town with steamships in the harbor. Everything about steam, coal and brass stays as written.

  • Fewest changes to the GDD and content.
  • Weaker guild flavor: guilds were fading by then.
  • Steamships and iron look less cozy than sails and lemon terraces.

With option A, only names and flavor text change. Item IDs are permanent, so steam_tonic keeps its ID even if players see "Lemon Tonic". Suggested renames:

Palette

Sea, whitewash and lemons

Today Guildie shares Hormiga's dark brown and brass. The new palette is the Amalfi coast at midday: a light, sea-teal world with whitewashed panels. Night mode is the same harbor after sunset, and the game can follow real time. Every text color here passes WCAG AA contrast on its background.

Type and icons

Chunky, warm, readable on a phone

Headings: Fraunces The Regatta starts Friday Everything else: Nunito Fill the spice merchant's order for 4 Healing Draughts before the galley sails. 1,250 ducats in the till.

Both are free Google fonts. The game would bundle them so it never depends on an outside server.

Item icons on majolica tiles

Rules: thick ink outline, two flat tones per material, one white highlight, seen slightly from above. The tile is always light, so icons read in both day and night. Later, icons can be rendered straight from the 3D models so the two always match.

Interaction

Every system gets a place and a verb

The rule: if the player does it, it happens somewhere in the harbor and their hands do something. Menus stay for reading numbers and making choices, but they open from the world. All of it sits on the server actions we already have, so the server still decides every result.

Technology

Three.js on top of what we have

My recommendation is to keep TypeScript, Vite, the PHP server and the database exactly as they are, and add a 3D layer in the browser. The menus stay as HTML because text, forms and keyboards work best there, and our phone and desktop tests keep working.

Menus and panelsHTML, React (a drop-in swap for Preact). Lists, forms, the sell screen, numbers.
open from taps in the world ↕ show results
The 3D harborThree.js through React Three Fiber. Buildings, people, ships, timers in the world, Focus moments.
both read the same game state
Game state and API clientThe current client code, unchanged.

OptionVerdict
Three.js + React Three FiberRecommended. The most used web 3D library, small, fast on phones, works with our build. AI sessions know it well, which matters because I'm the one writing it.
Babylon.jsGood full engine with built-in tools. About ten times heavier, and its own UI system fights with HTML menus.
PlayCanvasExcellent on phones and has a visual editor, but projects live in its cloud editor, which is hard to review in pull requests.
Godot 4 (web)We left it in 0.4.0: large download and weak at menus.
UnityRuled out.

Swapping Preact for React is about 35 KB more and almost no code change, because Preact copies React's API. React Three Fiber only runs on real React.

Budgets that keep it fast on a phone

Loads in two steps

Menus are usable in about a second, as today. The harbor streams in behind them, around 2–4 MB the first time, then cached.

Low-poly, one texture

Props up to 1,500 triangles, buildings up to 6,000. Most models share one small palette texture, so the colors stay on-brand and the phone draws the scene in few steps.

Kind to batteries

60 fps on a mid-range phone, 30 fps and no shadows on older ones. The scene stops drawing when nothing moves or the tab is hidden.

Making the art

How assets get made

You don't need Blender. The style is chunky low-poly with flat colors, which free kits and AI 3D tools already do well. I prepare every file (shrink it, recolor it to our palette, compress it) and wire it into the game.

Placeholders now

Free CC0 low-poly kits from Kenney (kenney.nl) and Quaternius (quaternius.com): harbor, pirate, nature and furniture sets. Free for commercial use, no credit needed. I can add these myself.

Hero pieces

Your bottega, the Mercato and the ships. Made in an AI image-to-3D tool such as Meshy, Tripo or Rodin from a concept image, then cleaned up by me.

Later, if the game grows

Hire a low-poly 3D artist for a final pass, using this page as the brief.

Your steps for one hero model

  1. Make a concept image

    In any image generator, paste the prompt below and change the first line to the thing you want. Pick the image you like best.

  2. Turn it into 3D

    In Meshy, Tripo or Rodin, choose "Image to 3D", upload the picture, and pick the low-poly or "stylized" option if there is one. Download the result as a .glb file.

  3. Hand it to me

    Drag the .glb into a new GitHub issue (or our chat) and name it after the object, like bottega_level1.glb. I'll shrink, recolor and place it, and you'll see it in a pull request preview.

A small Italian Renaissance craftsman's shop on a harbor quay. Low-poly 3D game asset, stylized and chunky like a cozy mobile builder game, isometric 3/4 view from above. Flat colors, no textures, soft daylight, simple shapes with bevelled edges. Plan

Getting there without stopping M2

  1. New look for the current menus

    Palette, fonts, icons and the Portovela names applied to today's screens. One small pull request, and the game stops looking like Hormiga right away. Also updates the GDD.

  2. 3D test slice

    Your bottega on the quay with the forge, the mine cart and the stall, using free placeholder models and the real game API. It is hidden behind a switch so the current game keeps working. You test it on your phone: does it feel good, and is it fast enough?

  3. Focus in 3D

    The bellows, stirring and stitching moments from the mock-up, with the server issuing and checking them. This is the M2 Focus prototype, done in the world instead of a menu.

  4. The whole harbor

    The Mercato, ships for events, NPC buyers walking the quay, other players' shops, and the morning return scene.

  5. Final art (M4)

    Hero models, item icons rendered from the models, sound, and the installable app.

What I need from you

  • Era: A (Renaissance, no steam) or B (steam age Riviera)?
  • City name: Portovela, or another?
  • Technology: OK to switch Preact to React and add Three.js?
  • Timing: start steps 1 and 2 now, beside M2, or wait?
  • Machine running cost: lamp oil, wages, or both?

Risks: 3D takes more work per feature than menus, and art is the slowest part. That's why step 2 is a test you can say no to after playing it. The menus never go away, so a phone that can't run 3D still plays the whole game.
